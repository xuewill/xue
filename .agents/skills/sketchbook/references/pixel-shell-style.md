# Pixel Shell Style

Use this mode only when the user supplies a source photo and explicitly asks for `pixel-shell`, `像素贝壳`, `贝壳像素`, or an equivalent material-pixel reconstruction.

## Source role

The uploaded photo is semantic evidence, not an output panel. Record its most memorable subject, basic composition, perspective relationship, structural direction, two to four visual anchors, overall atmosphere, and a discard list. Preserve recognition through those relationships while retaining no photographic pixels.

Never reproduce the original poster's upper-photo/lower-illustration split. The final result contains only the pixel-shell reconstruction across the physical Sketchbook pages.

## Construction

- Let module count adapt to the source subject's structural complexity; there is no numeric minimum or maximum.
- Modules are small square or near-square fragments with uniform or nearly uniform size, large enough to preserve material character and avoid noisy micro-tiling.
- Each fragment looks cut from rough shell, thin shell flakes, or natural mineral shell layers: slightly chipped edges, matte powdery grain, natural strata, mild relief, wear, and uneven color.
- The recognizable core is denser. Toward the boundary, modules progressively reduce, go missing, disconnect, and drift into negative space.
- Keep a clear geometric-grid order without becoming standard mosaic, digital pixels, game art, plastic tiles, glossy pearl, smooth vector blocks, tiny noise, or dots.

## Composition

- The material subject occupies about `30%–45%` of the usable two-page area; the rest is deliberate warm-paper negative space.
- Treat the subject like a small material seal: off-center, edge-adjacent, suspended, or restrainedly cropped according to the source direction and visual weight.
- Use positive/negative form, density, gathering/dispersion, rhythm, and asymmetric balance to create pause and breathing room.
- Keep the center binding readable and do not bisect the primary recognition anchor.
- The quiet pages remain active through paper grain and subtle material shadow, not through extra scenery, pasted fragments, or filler.

## Color

Extract the source photo's brightest, liveliest color relationships and recompose them rather than averaging or copying exact values. Favor clear sky blue, lake cyan, tender green, warm yellow, coral orange, peach pink, and other source-justified luminous colors. Use abundant clean warm white or a source-temperature near-white as breathing space.

Raise clarity, lightness, and chromatic vitality moderately while keeping the result warm and refined. Retain small natural shell variations without turning gray, dirty, aged, brown-filtered, muted Morandi, gloomy, neon, or cheaply candy-colored.

## Quality gate

- No source-photo panel or photographic pixels remain.
- Subject-to-source correspondence is recognizable through structure, direction, perspective, and visual anchors.
- Module density supports source recognition and material character without becoming dense micro-tiling or undifferentiated noise; no numeric count is required.
- Material reads as matte natural shell/mineral fragments, not standard mosaic or game pixels.
- The cluster occupies `30%–45%`; large negative space is intentional and balanced.
- Color is luminous and source-derived without gray aging, neon, or candy styling.
- The shared full-book silhouette, genuine exterior alpha, center binding, fixed upper marks, and contrast-aware location seal remain intact after composition.
