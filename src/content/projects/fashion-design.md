## Context

Fashion Design collects looks made for the Stanford FashionX Runway Show. The project is read as a set: the individual garments matter, but the runway sequence gives them their shared identity.

## Problem and constraints

A runway changes how a garment is seen. Silhouette arrives before detail, material reacts to light and movement, and each look is revealed from several angles in seconds. The five documented looks therefore needed variation without losing the sense that they belonged together.

## Role

I developed the documented fashion studies and organized them as a visual sequence for the show. The project uses the designer role consistently across the archive, while the related runway note supplies the surrounding design context.

## Key decisions and process

Each look needed a clear first impression and enough structure to remain interesting as it moved. Repeated ideas create continuity across the set; changes in proportion, volume, and emphasis keep the sequence from becoming one repeated shape. The image order preserves that variation as a readable record.

## Outcome

The project page preserves five looks as the primary visual record of the Stanford FashionX work. The companion note explains how movement, silhouette, and sequence shaped the presentation.
