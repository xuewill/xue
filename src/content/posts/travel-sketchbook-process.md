The homepage sketchbook moves through drawings of Osaka, Tokyo, Kyoto, Kamakura, Taipei, and Stanford. Together they form a small index of places rather than a conventional image carousel.

## Start with landmarks, keep the details

A landmark makes a location immediately legible, but the useful observation often sits around it: the density of a crossing, the angle of a roof, a street edge, a vehicle, or the way people occupy the foreground. Those details keep a page from feeling like a generic travel icon.

## Sequence creates a journey

The order of the drawings matters. Each automatic page turn should introduce a clear change in shape and place while still feeling like it belongs to the same book. Captions provide orientation without competing with the image.

## Preserve manual control

The sequence plays automatically when the homepage opens, then remains available for direct exploration. Pointer and keyboard controls let the reader move at a different pace, while reduced-motion preferences skip the physical page-turn animation and reveal a stable view.

The result treats motion as part of the archive's structure: it suggests the act of moving through pages without turning the drawings into decoration.
