The Fashion Design project collects looks made for the Stanford FashionX Runway Show. Seen together, the work is less about isolated garments than about maintaining a recognizable visual language across a moving sequence.

## Designing for movement

A runway changes how a design is read. Silhouette arrives before detail, material reacts to light and motion, and the front, side, and back are revealed in seconds. Each look therefore needs a clear first impression and enough structure to remain interesting as it passes.

## Variation without losing the set

The five documented looks use different proportions and details, but they need to feel related when presented in order. Repeated ideas create continuity; shifts in volume and emphasis keep the sequence from becoming one repeated shape.

## Keeping a visual record

The project page preserves the work as a simple image sequence. This article adds the design context around that sequence, while the project remains the primary visual record.
